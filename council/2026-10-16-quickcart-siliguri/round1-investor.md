Note: brief gives no vendor commission, so the *only* revenue line right now is the ₹25 delivery fee. Quick-commerce platforms typically also take 15–25% vendor commission [Fact, base.com: https://base.com/en-EN/blog/?p=19454; Storyboard18/CareEdge: https://www.storyboard18.com/amp/how-it-works/indias-quick-commerce-market-to-clock-rs-2-lakh-crore-in-3-years-careedge-report-73819.htm], but with only 3/5 vendors "interested" (not confirmed on terms), I can't assume one exists yet.

**Monthly fixed costs** [Assumption, low/likely/high]

| Item | Likely | Range |
|---|---|---|
| App/hosting/tools | ₹5,000 | ₹3k–8k |
| Marketing/customer acquisition (launch city, 10 vendors) | ₹15,000 | ₹10k–25k |
| Ops/admin (part-time support, phone, vendor coordination) | ₹10,000 | ₹8k–15k |
| **Total fixed** | **₹30,000/mo** | **₹21k–48k** |

(No separate rider fixed cost assumed — brief's ₹18/delivery is treated as fully variable gig pay, consistent with Siliguri-area rider gig pay data showing wide variance, e.g. ₹24k–32k/month fixed-shift riders in nearby Kolkata [Fact, Vahan: https://www.vahan.co/jobs/flipkart-minutes-delivery-boy-tegharia-kolkata/] — if QuickCart ever needs guaranteed shifts instead of per-order gig pay, this line reappears as a fixed cost.)

**Break-even:**
Units/month = Fixed costs ÷ Contribution margin = ₹30,000 ÷ ₹7 ≈ **4,286 orders/month** ≈ 143 orders/day ≈ **~14.3 orders/day per vendor** across 10 vendors. [Arithmetic from brief figures + assumption above]

**Cash to break-even:**
If volume ramps over ~3–4 months to reach 4,286 orders/month, cumulative fixed-cost burn ≈ ₹30,000 × 3.5 ≈ ₹105,000, plus one-time setup (app/MVP build, vendor onboarding, launch marketing) ≈ ₹50,000 [Assumption]. **Total: ₹100,000–200,000** before break-even. [Assumption, wide range because ramp speed is unknown]

**The number that breaks the model:**
**Cost per delivery.** Margin is ₹7/order — a buffer of just ₹7. If real-world cost per delivery (failed drop-offs, longer routes, fuel price swings, rider incentive pay) runs even ₹7–10 higher than the brief's ₹18 estimate — i.e., hits ~₹25–28 — contribution margin goes to zero or negative and no volume saves the model. This is the single most fragile input, since it's a self-reported estimate, not a measured one.

**My investment condition:** "I would invest if a 4-week pilot with the 3 confirmed vendors shows a measured (not estimated) average cost per delivery ≤ ₹20, at least 10 orders/vendor/day sustained, and COD collection losses/shortages under 2% of order value."

**Verdict from the numbers:** **Unattractive as described.** A ₹7 per-order margin with no vendor commission and an unverified cost assumption means break-even requires ~14 orders/vendor/day from a base of only 3 confirmed vendors — a big, unproven leap from current traction.
