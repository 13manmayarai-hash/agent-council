## Believer — Round 1
**Thesis:** A 10-vendor, cash-on-delivery grocery delivery pilot in Siliguri is a low-cost, low-risk way to test real local demand before committing to scale, and the unit economics already clear a positive contribution margin on day one.

**Top 3 reasons it can work**
1. The numbers already work before you add a single customer. ₹25 delivery fee minus ₹18 cost per delivery leaves ₹7 of positive contribution margin per order — [Fact] from the brief. That's not proof of a business, but it means the pilot doesn't need venture-scale volume to avoid bleeding cash on delivery itself; packing/vendor costs still need to be netted out, but the delivery leg alone isn't underwater — confidence 4.

2. Tier-2/3 Indian cities are where the quick-commerce growth is actually happening right now, and large players agree. Swiggy Instamart launched in Siliguri itself in February 2025 as part of a 100-city tier-2/3 push, and Swiggy's leadership said roughly one in four new Instamart users in 2025 came from tier-2/3 cities — [Fact] Swiggy Instamart press materials (swiggy.com/corporate, outlookbusiness.com, March 2025). This confirms real, current local demand in this exact city, not a hypothetical market — confidence 4.

3. Small-town delivery economics tend to be more forgiving, not less. Zomato has stated delivery costs can run up to 50% lower in non-metro cities versus metros due to shorter distances and lower wages, and Emkay research cited breakeven around ~800 orders/store in tier-2 cities versus ~1,300 in metros — [Fact] officechai.com reporting on Zomato statements; Emkay figures via secondary source (verify original) — confidence 3. A scrappy COD pilot with local riders is well-suited to exploit exactly this advantage, and COD removes a real adoption barrier (digital payment trust) for a first-time local customer base — [Assumption] confidence 3.

**Best realistic outcome:** The pilot validates demand and ops with 10 vendors, proves delivery can run at a small per-order profit before overhead, and gives enough data (order frequency, basket size, vendor reliability) to justify raising vendor count and seeking a city-wide or funded expansion — [Assumption].

**What would prove me wrong:** If average basket size is too small to generate repeat orders at ₹25 fee economics, or if only 3 of 5 vendors staying interested signals weak vendor-side commitment that collapses supply before demand is even tested — [Assumption] this is the single biggest risk to the thesis.
