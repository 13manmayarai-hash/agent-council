## Skeptic — Round 1
**Biggest single reason this fails:** the ₹7 gross margin per order (₹25 fee − ₹18 delivery cost) has to absorb cash-handling losses, returns, vendor commission, and the founder's own time before a single rupee of profit exists — it likely can't.

**Risks**
1. Unit economics are razor-thin and probably negative once real costs are counted — [Fact from brief] ₹25 delivery fee − ₹18 cost per delivery = ₹7 contribution, before COD cash reconciliation, packaging, RTO/refused orders, vendor commission, app/ops costs, or marketing — severity 5.
   Cheapest test (≤2 weeks): Run a real (not hypothetical) 2-week pilot with the 3 interested vendors, logging every cost — cash shortfalls, redelivery, wasted trips — against actual revenue. If true contribution per order is below ~₹3-5 after these, the model needs a fee/cost redesign before scaling.

2. Vendor "interest" is weak and unconfirmed — [Fact from brief] 3 of 5 vendors approached expressed interest in casual conversation; none are described as signed or committed, and the target is 10 vendors (only 3 of the needed 10 even contacted favorably) — severity 4.
   Cheapest test (≤1 week): Get a written/verbal commitment with specifics (commission %, who restocks inventory data, exclusivity) from all 10 target vendors. If fewer than 7-8 convert from "interested" to "committed," demand on the supply side isn't there yet.

3. Cash-on-delivery carries a meaningful fraud/refusal tax that a thin-margin model can't absorb — [Fact] Indian COD orders see ~28–35% RTO/return rates vs 4–8% for prepaid (shipmozo.com COD fraud guide) — severity 4.
   Cheapest test (≤2 weeks): During the pilot, track refusal/no-show/short-cash incidents as a % of orders. Even a 10% refusal rate at this margin erases most profit; if it tracks toward the 28-35% industry figure, COD-only is not viable as designed.

4. National quick-commerce players may already cover or soon enter Siliguri with faster, prepaid service — [Fact] Blinkit, Zepto, and Instamart together hold ~90% of India's quick-commerce market and are expanding into 25+ cities each; [Guess] whether any already serve Siliguri specifically is unconfirmed — severity 3.
   Cheapest test (<1 day, free): Check Blinkit/Zepto/Instamart app pincode coverage for Siliguri addresses directly. If any already deliver there, QuickCart's slower COD model has a tough differentiation problem.

5. No stated capacity to run daily ops (cash reconciliation, substitutions, complaints, vendor coordination) alone — [Assumption, not stated in brief] severity 3.
   Cheapest test (≤1 week): Manually run 10-15 simulated orders/day for a week and time every task end-to-end to find the real bottleneck before any app/hiring investment.

**What would change my mind:** a 2-week pilot showing real per-order contribution margin ≥₹8-10 after cash losses/returns, ≥7 vendors with real signed terms, and confirmation that no major quick-commerce app currently serves the target Siliguri neighborhoods.
