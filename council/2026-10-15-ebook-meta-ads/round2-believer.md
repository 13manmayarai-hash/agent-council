## Believer — Rebuttal

**Concede**
- **Selling one e-book to cold ad traffic loses money.** The Investor's numbers match my own Round 1. I estimated $33–$100 in ads per buyer. The Investor gets about $50 against $15.36 net per sale, and even the best case ($22.50) loses money [Fact, from round1-investor.md]. I agree with the Skeptic's main point: the backend that makes this work does not exist yet.
- **My test budget was too small to prove profit.** $300–$500 can show whether ads get clicks and sales at all. It cannot produce the 40+ purchases the Investor wants before calling it break-even. The Investor's $2,000–$5,000 to reach break-even is the more honest total [Assumption].
- **A free lead magnet is not a cheap way around this.** I checked: US Facebook lead campaigns averaged about $27.66 per lead in 2025 ([WordStream/LocaliQ](https://www.wordstream.com/blog/facebook-ads-benchmarks-2025)), and one small agency sample put ecommerce at about $27 ([Focus Digital](https://focus-digital.co/average-cost-per-lead-on-facebook-july-2025-report/)) [Fact]. "Just build a list" is not free either.
- **The Skeptic's other risks are real.** Meta can disable a new ad account, and the FTC takes action over "make money" claims [Fact, sources in round1-skeptic.md].

**Counter**
1. **The real decision is a staged test, not a $3,000 bet.** [Assumption] The Investor's workable scenario (CPC $1, 5% conversion, $30 average order) gives about +$7 per sale [Fact, from round1-investor.md]. Whether this niche can get there is unknown, but checking costs little. The first $300–$500 tests the leading signals. The Skeptic proposes a click-through rate of 1% or more as the bar, and the cost per sale should be within about 2x of order value. If those fail, stop and lose only that money. Only if they pass should the user spend the rest. The downside is capped at each gate.
2. **The free-substitute risk can be checked for free before writing anything.** [Assumption] Free content mostly hurts generic topics. People pay for a packaged, specific outcome, such as "a 30-day career-switch plan for teachers." The Skeptic's own Meta Ad Library check settles this at no cost. If competitors' ads in a niche have run for 3 months or more, those advertisers are probably profitable. If no niche shows that, I would drop the idea.
3. **The account-ban and legal risks are about topic choice, not reasons to quit.** [Assumption] Pick productivity or careers rather than health or money. Write copy with no income or results promises. Sell through a platform that handles VAT, such as Payhip or Lemon Squeezy. Collect emails from the first day. The Skeptic suggested each of these. Together they reduce, but do not remove, the risk of depending on Meta alone.

**Updated confidence in the idea: 2 out of 5 (down from about 3).** All three Round 1 analyses agree that the first sale loses money at benchmark rates. The case therefore depends on a backend that hasn't been built and conversion rates that haven't been shown. I still think a capped, gated test is worth running, because it costs a few hundred dollars and has clear stop rules. But "worth pursuing as a business" is not supported until a test shows ad cost per buyer at or below average order value.
