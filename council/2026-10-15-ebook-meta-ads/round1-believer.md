## Believer — Round 1

**Thesis:** Selling how-to e-books through Meta ads is a cheap, fast, low-risk way to test demand. It is worth pursuing if it is run as a small, disciplined experiment and treated as the front end of a funnel. Selling one $9 book per ad click will not work on its own.

**Top 3 reasons it can work**

1. **Almost every sale is margin, and failing costs little.** [Fact, from the brief] The user plans how-to / self-help e-books in fitness, money, productivity and careers, sold in $ or £. [Assumption] A digital file has no inventory, shipping or production cost per copy. After writing it, the main costs are the payment processor fee and ad spend. That means the realistic downside is capped at a test budget the user chooses, plus their writing time. Few businesses let a beginner test a paid channel with no stock risk. — confidence 4

2. **Clicks are cheap enough to test quickly, and these topics are proven ad categories.** [Fact] 2025 benchmarks put average Meta CPC at about $0.70–$1.40, depending on the source and how it's defined. One source drawn from 5,200+ ad accounts reports a $0.82 CPC and a $5.97 CPM ([WordStream](https://www.wordstream.com/blog/facebook-ads-benchmarks-2025), [TwoMinuteReports](https://twominutereports.com/blog/facebook-ads-benchmarks)). One benchmark lists fitness at a $1.90 CPC with a high conversion rate, but its method isn't stated, so treat that as directional only ([bestever.ai](https://www.bestever.ai/post/facebook-ads-benchmarks)). [Assumption] At about $1 a click, $300–$500 buys a few hundred clicks. That is enough to learn whether a hook and offer convert, within weeks rather than months. — confidence 3

3. **Making ads and targeting them is easier for a solo seller now than it used to be.** [Fact] In 2025 Meta launched generative-AI creative tools under Advantage+, including turning images into video, aimed at small advertisers. Meta itself claims Advantage+ Sales campaigns give an average 22% lift in return on ad spend, but that is Meta's figure, not an independent one ([eMarketer](https://www.emarketer.com/content/meta-launches-11-new-ai-ad-tools-cannes-lions-2025), [Search Engine Land](https://searchengineland.com/meta-generative-ai-tools-automated-video-branding-creative-ads-457221)). [Assumption] Testing many ad variations no longer needs a designer or a media buyer, and that removes the main barrier for someone starting from scratch. — confidence 3

**The mechanism (stated honestly):** [Assumption] The idea only works if the money comes from the whole funnel, not the first sale. The structure would be:
- a $7–$17 e-book as the entry offer;
- an add-on at checkout (a workbook or templates);
- a higher-priced follow-up offer (a course or bundle);
- an email list the user can sell to again for free.

[Guess] At about $1 per click and a 1–3% landing-page conversion rate, getting one buyer costs roughly $33–$100. A single $9 sale loses money at that cost. The funnel has to average about $30–$60 per buyer to break even on the first purchase.

**Best realistic outcome:** [Guess] After 2–4 months of testing, one niche (for example career change or a specific fitness goal) breaks even or slightly profits on the first purchase, and its email list grows. That becomes a small side income of a few hundred to a few thousand dollars a month, plus a buyer list and proven ad angles that can be reused for higher-priced products. A large standalone business is possible but is not the base case.

**What would prove me wrong:** A $300–$500 test across 3–5 ad angles that cannot get the cost of one buyer under about 2x the funnel's average revenue per buyer, with no improvement trend. That would mean Meta's auction is too crowded for this niche, and the case collapses.
