# Council Verdict: Selling how-to / self-help e-books through Facebook and Instagram ads

**Verdict:** NO-GO
**Confidence:** 4
**One-line reason:** At benchmark rates, every member's numbers show a cheap e-book costs about 2.4 to 5.5 times more in ads to sell than it earns. The only version that could work needs a higher-priced follow-up product, and the user hasn't built or tested one.

## What the evidence shows
- **Strongest point for:** Failure is cheap and can be capped. A digital file has no inventory or per-copy cost, so a staged test can stop the loss at about $600 ($500 in ads plus about $100 in tools) before any big commitment (Believer R1 [Assumption], conceded by Skeptic R2, made concrete by Investor R2 as arithmetic). This shows the test is cheap. It doesn't show the business is good.
- **Strongest point against:** The cost of ads per sale is far above the money each sale brings in. A $17 book nets $14.01 to $15.36 after fees (Payhip vs Gumroad) [Fact-based arithmetic, Investor]. At a sourced $1.24 cost per click and a 1.6 to 2.5% conversion rate, ads cost **$49.60 to $77.50 per sale** (Investor R1/R2). The 1.6% figure is the best-sourced conversion number presented: Triple Whale data from about 35,000 accounts, via WordStream (Skeptic R1, [Fact]). To break even, a single book would need 8.1 to 8.9% of visitors to buy. A funnel with a checkout add-on and an upsell would still need 5.9%. The Investor's own benchmark band for cold traffic tops out at 5%. **Nobody disputed this.** The Believer conceded it outright in R2.
- **Unsupported claims set aside:**
  - Believer's "best realistic outcome" of a few hundred to a few thousand dollars a month [Guess, no basis].
  - Believer's claim that buyers will pay for a "packaged, specific outcome" despite free substitutes [Assumption, untested].
  - Meta's claim that Advantage+ lifts return on ad spend by 22% [vendor's own figure, not independent]. Even taken at face value it only lifts return per ad dollar from 0.43 to 0.52, against the 1.0 needed to break even.
  - The fitness cost per click of $1.90 and its "high conversion" [source doesn't state its method].
  - The Investor's best-case cost per click of $0.90 [no source; the sourced low is $0.82].
  - The order-add-on and upsell take rates (30% and 10%) and all fixed costs [Assumption].
  - The "Ad Library ads running 3+ months means the advertiser is profitable" rule (Skeptic) [a reasonable heuristic, but no evidence offered for it].
  - The account-ban risk rests on one forum thread [Fact, but anecdotal]. It is plausible, but its frequency is unknown.

**Arithmetic check (Investor):**
- Most figures check out: the $1.64 fee, the $15.36 net, the CAC values ($49.60 / $126.67 / $22.50 / $77.50 / $32.80), the $23.40 average order, the 5.9% / 8.1% / 8.9% break-even conversion rates, the $2.99 Gumroad fee, the 14 sales a month in the workable scenario, and the 2.4 to 3.7x gap.
- Applying a flat 9.7% fee to the $23.40 order slightly overstates fees. The true net is about $21.25, not $21.13. This makes no material difference.
- **Error in the investment condition** (Investor R1, repeated by Skeptic R2). "At least 40 purchases from $1,000 at a CAC of $20 or less" contradicts itself: 40 purchases from $1,000 is a $25 CAC. **Corrected: at least 50 purchases from $1,000** (or 40 purchases from no more than $800).
- **"Backend of 2.5–4x a $17 book."** Revenue per buyer must reach $55 to $86 gross, which is **about 3.2 to 5 times the book price in total**. The add-on revenue on top of the book is about 2.2 to 4 times. The Investor's range is only right on the second reading.
- **Stage 1 pass bar too loose.** Passing Stage 1 at 10 purchases from $500 means a $50 CAC. That is still 2.4 times above the $21 net order, and Stage 2 then needs CAC to fall to $20. A pass at $50 is a weak signal, not a green light. See the tests below.

## The single biggest risk
**Unit economics: CAC against revenue per buyer.** This is a known bad result at benchmark rates, not an open question. All three members' numbers independently put the ad cost per sale above net revenue in every scenario, including the Investor's best case ($22.50 against $15.36). The fixes offered (cheaper clicks, Advantage+, add-ons and upsells) close only a small part of a 2.4x or larger gap. Only one thing could close it: a backend product (course, coaching or bundle) worth $100+ that a meaningful share of buyers takes up. That is a different business, with its own build cost and credibility problem, and the founder has stated no expertise in any niche (brief). Account bans, free substitutes and legal exposure only matter if this risk is solved first.

## Conditions (if GO WITH CONDITIONS)
Not applicable: the verdict is NO-GO. The tests below are the cheap way to earn a re-hearing. They are not a softer yes.

## Three cheapest next tests (≤ 2 weeks each)
| Test | How | Result that means continue | Result that means stop |
|---|---|---|---|
| 1. Niche demand check (free) | Search Meta Ad Library for 3 to 5 narrow topics, ideally productivity or careers, where ad review is lighter than health or money. Count active paid e-book/guide offers and how long each has run. | At least one niche with several advertisers whose ads have run 3+ months, but not saturated (well under ~50). | No niche shows long-running paid offers, or every niche is saturated. |
| 2. Backend pre-sale (about $0 to $50) | Write a one-page offer for a $100+ follow-up product in the chosen niche. Pre-sell it to about 20 people from any warm source (friends, communities, LinkedIn). Do this before writing the e-book or spending on ads. | At least 2 to 3 paid pre-orders. That is the first evidence that revenue per buyer could reach $55 to $86. | Zero paid pre-orders. The funnel maths has no basis, so don't run ads. |
| 3. Capped ad probe (about $500 + about $100 tools; only if tests 1 and 2 pass) | Landing page plus checkout on a merchant-of-record platform. No income or result claims in the copy. About 10 creative variants. Collect emails from day one. | Click-through rate of at least 1% and a CAC of about $35 or less, trending down. Only then go to Stage 2: **at least 50 purchases from $1,000** (CAC $20 or less), with front-end ROAS of at least 1.2. | Click-through rate under 1%, or fewer than 10 purchases from $500 (CAC above $50). Also stop on any account disablement you can't appeal. |

## What would flip this verdict
- **To GO WITH CONDITIONS:** a pre-sold backend offer (test 2) showing that revenue per buyer can plausibly reach about $55 to $86 gross, **plus** a niche with long-running competitor ads (test 1). Those two together would turn the gap from known bad into a question one paid test can settle.
- **To GO:** a paid test showing CAC at or below net revenue per buyer at volume, meaning at least 50 purchases from $1,000 at a CAC of $20 or less, with the email list growing.
- **To a firm NO-GO with higher confidence:** test 2 produces no pre-sales, or test 3 shows a click-through rate under 1% and a CAC above $50.

## Where the council agreed / disagreed
- **Agreed:**
  - A single cheap e-book sold to cold Meta traffic loses money on every sale (all three; the Believer conceded in R2).
  - The idea depends entirely on a backend that doesn't exist.
  - A $300 to $500 test can rule the idea out but cannot prove profit: it buys only about 6 to 10 sales (Believer and Skeptic conceded to the Investor).
  - The real cash needed to break even, if it works at all, is $2,000 to $5,000 over 2 to 4 months.
  - Build an email list from day one.
  - Avoid income claims.
  - Use a platform that handles UK VAT.
- **Still disputed:**
  - Whether running the capped test is worth the money at all. The Believer and Investor say yes, at about $600. The Skeptic says pre-sell the backend first. I side with the Skeptic on the order: an ad test without a backend measures an offer that loses money by design.
  - How likely Meta account bans are, and how much they hurt. The evidence is anecdotal and unresolved; the email list is the only hedge offered.
  - Whether free YouTube and AI content suppresses conversion for specific, narrow guides. This hasn't been tested yet, and test 1 partly addresses it.
