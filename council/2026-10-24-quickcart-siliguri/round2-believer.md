## Believer — Rebuttal

**Concede:**
- Customer demand is unproven. The brief has no customer, no problem and no buyer conversations, only 3 of 5 vendors "interested". [Fact] from the brief. My Round 1 reason 2 was about the supply side only.
- My margin was too rosy. The ₹7 is before refusals and misc costs. The Investor's ₹4.75 likely case (₹23.75 effective revenue minus ₹19) is more realistic, and the arithmetic holds. Break-even near 70 orders/day on ₹10k fixed costs is a high bar for a cold start. [Assumption] on the inputs, as the Investor tagged them.
- My "gap in a smaller city" point (reason 3) is weakened. The Investor cites Blinkit in 153 cities by March 2025 (Wikipedia, via the Investor; I could not open pages to re-check). Neither the Skeptic nor I could confirm Siliguri coverage. I now treat that point as unproven, confidence 1-2.
- The ₹18 is an unmeasured estimate. The Investor's check is right: ₹18 only holds at roughly 32 deliveries per rider-day on a ₹15k wage [Assumption: wage].
- ₹40k covers the likely case with no reserve. That is the Investor's estimate, an [Assumption], but I have no better one.

**Counter:**
1. **"Don't spend the ₹40k" does not rule out a launch; it rules out a big-bang launch.** The Skeptic's own cheapest test is WhatsApp orders from about 30 households, with no app and no hired riders. [Assumption] That costs a small fraction of ₹40k, perhaps a few thousand rupees for fuel and flyers (my estimate; no source). The risk is capped, and it answers the demand question directly. So the question is not "launch with 10 vendors or not". It is "run a 2-week, 3-vendor pilot, then decide". The brief's 3 interested vendors fit that exactly.
2. **The weak margin is a pricing-model problem, and the Investor already showed the fix.** With a 5% commission on a ₹400 basket, the Investor's figures give about ₹25 margin per order and break-even near 13 orders/day instead of about 70. [Assumption] The ₹400 basket and the 5% rate are untested; no Siliguri grocery AOV was found. Vendors are paying nothing today, so a commission is a real ask. Even so, a fee-only model is not the only version of this idea. A pilot should test both the ₹25 fee and a commission.
3. **COD leakage can be capped by design.** The Skeptic's controls are cheap: cap pilot orders at about ₹500, settle vendors daily by UPI after collecting cash, and use known neighbourhoods. [Assumption] These should hold exposure well under ₹5k at pilot scale; I have no COD loss data for Siliguri. COD is also the likely default payment mode for a first-time local service [Guess], so removing it would cost orders. The Investor's 24% refusal threshold at ₹18 cost is a useful tripwire to track from day one.

**Updated confidence in the idea:** 2/5 as posed (10 vendors, fee-only, full ₹40k). 3/5 as a staged pilot with a commission test. Before the rebuttal I held roughly 3 for the original. I dropped it because the customer evidence gap, the thinner margin and my weaker competition point all stand. The pilot version stays at 3 because its downside is small and the kill criteria are clear. Concretely, I'd agree with the Investor's pilot gates (≥40 orders/day is probably too high for 2 weeks, so I'd use the Skeptic's repeat-order test first), a measured all-in cost at or below ₹18, and at least 3 vendors committed in writing.
