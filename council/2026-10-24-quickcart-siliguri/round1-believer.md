## Believer — Round 1
**Thesis:** A tiny, cheap-to-test hyperlocal delivery service for existing Siliguri grocers can work as a low-risk pilot, because it adds a delivery channel vendors lack and the unit economics are positive on paper.

**Top 3 reasons it can work**
1. Positive delivery margin and a small downside. Fee ₹25 vs cost ₹18 leaves ₹7 per delivery (28% of the fee). Savings at risk are ₹40,000. — [Fact] from the brief (the figures are the user's own). [Assumption] that ₹18 includes rider pay, fuel and cash-handling losses; the brief doesn't say. Breakeven on a fixed cost is about 143 deliveries per ₹1,000. Confidence 3.
2. Vendors want it. 3 of 5 vendors interviewed were interested, so the supply side is not blank. — [Fact] from the brief. [Assumption] "interested" is not a commitment; the sample is five. Vendors who already have loyal walk-in and phone customers can add delivery without new inventory or dark stores, which keeps capital needs low. Confidence 3.
3. Possible gap in a smaller city. National quick-commerce players are concentrated in big metros. A 2024 snapshot lists Instamart at 43 cities and Zepto at 10+, and my search found no reporting of a Siliguri launch. — [Fact] https://www.indiaretailing.com/2024/11/22/blinkit-swiggy-instamart-zepto-redefining-shopping/ (search snippet only; I couldn't open the page, and the data is 2024). [Assumption] that Siliguri is still underserved in Oct 2026; my search was thin and I can't confirm. Confidence 2.

**Why now / edge:** [Assumption] Consumers across India are now used to app and phone delivery, so a local alternative doesn't need to teach the habit. [Guess] A Siliguri-based founder knows the markets and vendors, and cash on delivery matches local payment habits. The brief doesn't state the founder's background, so this is unverified.

**Best realistic outcome:** A small, profitable local operation. For example, 10 vendors at about 30 deliveries a day each is 300 deliveries and about ₹2,100 a day in margin before fixed costs. [Guess] That figure is an illustration, not a forecast. It's enough to prove demand and fund a part-time rider team, or to set up a later raise or a vendor-subscription model. It won't be a venture-scale business on delivery fees alone.

**What would prove me wrong:** A two-week pilot with 3 committed vendors that shows fewer than about 10 orders a day in total, or a true per-delivery cost (including failed COD orders, rider idle time and cash leakage) above ₹25. Either would show the ₹7 margin doesn't exist.

**Gaps in the brief:** The customer and the problem are not defined. Neither is who pays for rider labor, how COD cash is reconciled, or what the vendors' own pricing and margins are.
