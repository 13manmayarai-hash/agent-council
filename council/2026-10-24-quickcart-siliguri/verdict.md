# Council Verdict: QuickCart Siliguri

**Verdict:** TEST FIRST
**Confidence:** 3
**One-line reason:** Don't launch the 10-vendor, fee-only version with the full ₹40,000. Customer demand, the true cost per delivery and local competition are all still unknown, and a WhatsApp pilot costing under ₹5,000 can answer all three in two weeks.
**Evidence warning:** Opening web pages did not work this session. I tried one cited source (Outlook Business) and it failed, so every cited web source is marked not reachable. The verdict rests mainly on the brief's own numbers and the Investor's arithmetic, which I checked. It does not rely on any outside source. Nobody could confirm whether Blinkit, Zepto or Instamart deliver in Siliguri.

## What the evidence shows
- **Strongest point for:** The downside can be capped. Three vendors are already interested, and a pilot with no app and no hired riders can test demand for a small part of the ₹40k. All members accepted this in rebuttal. (Believer, Round 2; Skeptic conceded it; brief counts are [Fact], the pilot cost is [Assumption].)
- **Strongest point against:** With the ₹25 fee as the only income, the margin is about ₹4.75 per order. Break-even is about 70 orders a day, and the ₹18 cost only holds if each rider makes about 32 drops a day. If QuickCart pays vendors before collecting the cash, the cash needed to reach break-even is about ₹63k (range ₹56k to ₹77k). That is more than the ₹40,000 available. (Investor, Rounds 1 and 2. The fee and cost are [Fact] from the brief. Refusal rate, fixed costs, wage and basket size are [Assumption].) I am not treating this as a known-bad blocker, because every input apart from the fee and cost is an assumption. It does show the plan as briefed is fragile.
- **The fix the council found:** a vendor commission of 4% or more, plus vendors carrying the COD credit (they are paid only after the rider hands over the cash). Together these bring break-even to roughly 13 to 16 orders a day and the cash need back to about ₹35k. The commission is not in the brief, and no vendor has been asked about it. ([Assumption], Investor; Skeptic and Believer agreed it is the key lever.)
- **Unsupported claims set aside:** the Believer's illustration of 10 vendors × 30 deliveries a day = 300 a day [Guess]. Siliguri as an underserved "gap in a smaller city", which the Believer conceded down to confidence 1-2. The founder's local edge [Guess, not in the brief]. Cash on delivery as the likely default payment habit [Guess]. The Investor's 5% COD refusal rate and ₹1 misc cost per order [Assumption, no source]. The ₹400 average basket [Assumption, no Siliguri data]. The two job ads (Blinkit and Zepto hiring in Siliguri) only hint that the apps are there. They don't show delivery coverage.

**Arithmetic check (Investor):** all correct.
- 25 × 0.95 − 19 = ₹4.75.
- ₹10,000 ÷ ₹4.75 ≈ 2,105 orders a month, about 70 a day.
- ₹15,000 ÷ 26 days ÷ ₹18 ≈ 32 drops a day.
- Margin hits zero when the delivery cost reaches ₹22.75, about 25.4 drops a day on a ₹15k wage.
- At ₹18 cost, the COD refusal rate that breaks even is 24%.
- With a 4% commission on a ₹400 basket, margin is ₹20.75 and break-even is about 482 orders a month.
- Cash needed: ₹35k plus ₹21k to ₹42k of float = ₹56k to ₹77k.
- Payback at 80 a day takes more than 2 years (₹40k ÷ about ₹1,400 a month ≈ 29 months).

The Skeptic's "more than 1 order every 20 minutes" for 30 orders over 10 hours is actually exactly one every 20 minutes. That slip doesn't change anything.

## The single biggest risk
**Nobody has shown that customers will order repeatedly and pay ₹25.** The brief has no defined customer and no customer conversations. Every break-even figure (70 a day fee-only, about 13 a day with commission) depends on order volume that has never been observed. If demand is not there, no change to pricing or settlement saves the business. That is why the Skeptic kept it at severity 5 and nobody disputed it.

## Three cheapest next tests (≤ 2 weeks each)
| Test | How | Result that means continue | Result that means stop |
|---|---|---|---|
| 1. Demand pilot | Take WhatsApp orders from about 30 households near the 3 interested vendors. Charge ₹25, use cash on delivery, cap orders at ₹500, deliver yourself, no app. Spend at most ₹5,000. Log every order, refusal and cost, including idle time. | 10 or more households place a second order within 2 weeks. COD refusals stay at or below 5%. The measured all-in cost is at or below ₹18 per delivery, or below ₹22 if a commission is agreed. | Fewer than 10 repeat customers, or a measured all-in cost of ₹25 or more |
| 2. Competition check (about 1 hour) | Enter pincodes 734001 and 734003 in Blinkit, Zepto, Instamart, BigBasket and JioMart. Note coverage, fee and delivery time. Ask 5 vendors whether they already deliver and what they charge. | Clear gaps exist (outer wards, small baskets, evening slots, or items the apps don't stock), or vendors don't deliver themselves | Apps cover your target areas with free or cheap delivery AND vendors already deliver for free |
| 3. Vendor terms | Ask the 3 interested vendors to agree in writing (WhatsApp is fine) to a 2-week pilot with: a commission of 4% or more on the order value, payment to the vendor only after the rider hands over the cash (or same-day UPI), set packing times, and rules for out-of-stock items | 3 vendors sign the commission and post-collection settlement, and the out-of-stock rate in the pilot stays under 15% | Fewer than 3 commit, or none will pay any commission. That leaves you on the fee-only model, which needs about 70 orders a day and more cash than you have. |

## What would flip this verdict
- **To GO WITH CONDITIONS:** all three tests pass. That means 10 or more repeat customers, a measured all-in cost at or below ₹18 (or a commission covering the gap), refusals at or below 5%, 3 or more vendors signed to a commission of 4% or more with post-collection settlement, and a real gap in app coverage. Even then, grow from 3 vendors toward 10 in stages. Put more of the ₹40k in only when daily orders head toward the commission-model break-even of about 15 a day.
- **To NO-GO:** the apps already cover Siliguri with cheap delivery and vendors deliver free. Or the measured cost is ₹23 or more with no commission. Or households don't reorder. Any one of these is a real blocker.

## Where the council agreed / disagreed
- **Agreed:**
  - Customer demand is unproven.
  - ₹7 overstates the margin, and ₹4.75 is the better estimate.
  - The ₹18 cost is unmeasured.
  - The fee-only plan with the full ₹40k is unattractive or marginal.
  - A cheap WhatsApp pilot with 3 vendors should come first.
  - A vendor commission and vendors carrying the COD credit are the main levers.
  - Competition in Siliguri is unconfirmed either way.
- **Still disputed:**
  - What the pilot's success bar should be. The Investor wants 40 or more orders a day within 4 weeks; the Believer and Skeptic want the repeat-order test first. Use the repeat-order test for the first 2 weeks, then the Investor's bar before scaling.
  - Whether vendors will accept any commission at all. No one has asked them yet.
