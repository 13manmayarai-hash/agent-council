**Break-even**
- Likely: ₹10,000 ÷ ₹4.75 = **about 2,105 orders/month (about 70/day)**
- Optimistic: ₹5,000 ÷ ₹6 = about 833/month (about 28/day)
- Pessimistic: margin is negative, so **there is no break-even. More orders means bigger losses.**
- If the founder pays himself ₹15k: 25,000 ÷ 4.75 = about 5,260/month (about 175/day)

**Cash to break-even: about ₹40k likely (₹15k–₹60k+)**
- One-time setup (branding, bags, COD float): ₹10k (₹5k–₹15k) [Assumption]
- Ramp from 0 to 70/day over 6 months [Assumption]. Average contribution is about half of fixed costs, so net burn is about ₹5k/month × 6 = ₹30k.
- Total: about ₹40k. That uses up the whole ₹40,000 [Fact, brief] with nothing in reserve. Any launch discounts come on top of this.

**The number that breaks the model:** the all-in cost per delivery. Each ₹1 of extra cost removes about 20% of the margin. At about **₹22.75 cost + ₹1 misc (₹23.75 total), at 5% refusals, the margin hits zero.** That happens if riders average under about 26 drops/day on a ₹15k wage. Close second: a COD refusal rate above about 24% kills it even at ₹18. A third gap: the brief says 10 vendors, but only 3 of 5 are "interested" [Fact, brief].

**Upside lever:** a 5% vendor commission on a ₹400 basket [Assumption: AOV ₹300–₹600; I found no Siliguri grocery AOV] adds ₹20/order. The margin rises to about ₹25 and break-even drops to about 400 orders/month (about 13/day).

**My investment condition:** "I would invest if a 4-week pilot shows ≥40 orders/day, a *measured* all-in delivery cost ≤₹18, COD refusals ≤5%, and at least 3 vendors signed in writing to a ≥4% commission."

**Verdict from the numbers:** **Marginal, leaning Unattractive.** With a ₹25 fee as the only revenue, the margin is under ₹5, break-even needs about 70 orders/day, and the ₹40k only covers the likely case if everything goes to plan. Adding a vendor commission is what would make it investable.
